title: Is the Stuff You Produce Actually Valuable
lede: It's easy to make useful looking stuff, but producing something that your users truly appreciate requires skill, insight and taste.
---

We've all been there. Sitting in a meeting looking at a slide where everything seems to be in order, and even of very high quality. But the confusion is still there: What am I, or anybody, supposed to get out of this? What is that person trying to say with this? Why are there so many graphs and numbers on this slide, what should I focus on?

This is what happens when people are evaluated on, or more often they evaluate themselves based on how much output they can produce. AI tools are able to gather a bunch of data, create graphs, compile them to a hundred slides and make them pretty with a couple of prompts if that. If you think producing more equals, or even roughly correlates, with the value you bring to your team, it's obvious that you will start to create more stuff.

But this is where you miss the point; you don't see that your value is in the key insights you find, not the hundred data points you gather. It's way more impactful to find the most crucial, most impactful idea and focus on it. Look at it from different sides, evaluate possibilities for creating change you think is worthwhile. Communicate your reasoning and your vision of what the situation should be. Formulate a plan, evaluate what you need, gather the resources to get that done and execute.

This isn't solely an AI slop issue either. There always have been people who think the purpose of their job is to recite numbers from a dashboard, the og meat proxies. But AI has made this issue way worse in two ways. First, it removes the effort from producing stuff, so people will just produce more. Secondly, it'll make you feel like you know the material, both by making things look like they make sense as well as simply by glazing you until you're sure you've got it. You probably don't.

Next time you're about to present something, just ask yourself "If I was allowed only a single data point or a single sentence to explain this, what would it be?"
