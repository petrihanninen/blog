---
title: Self-Hosting Was Fun Until It Wasn't
date: 2025-09-08
lede: Three years of running my own cluster, and what finally made me pull the plug.
---

For three years this blog, a few side projects and an embarrassing amount of monitoring ran on a small Kubernetes cluster in my hallway closet. Three mini PCs, a cheap switch, and a lot of YAML.

I learned more from that closet than from any job. I also spent more weekends on it than I would like to admit.

### The good part

Everything was mine. I understood every layer, from the kernel to the ingress controller. When something broke I could fix it, and something broke often enough that I got good at fixing things. That experience paid for itself many times over at work.

The deployment for this blog looked roughly like this:

```yaml
apiVersion: apps/v1
kind: Deployment
metadata:
  name: blog
spec:
  replicas: 2
  template:
    spec:
      containers:
        - name: blog
          image: registry.local/blog:latest
          ports:
            - containerPort: 3000
```

Forty more lines for the service, the ingress, the certificate and the secrets. Multiply by every project.

### The bad part

The closet does not care that it is Sunday. A power cut at 03:00 meant a dead etcd quorum and a morning of recovery. A kernel update meant a node that would not rejoin. My partner learned the phrase "is the cluster down again" and used it more than I would like.

The honest accounting: the cluster cost me roughly a weekend a month. For a blog with a few hundred readers.

### What I moved to

A managed platform that deploys from a `git push`. The entire configuration is a single file and I have not touched it since. I lost the ability to tinker and gained all of my weekends back.

I do not regret the three years. I would not do them again either. The closet is now just a closet, and I am surprisingly fine with that.
