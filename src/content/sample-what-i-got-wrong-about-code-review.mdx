---
title: What I Got Wrong About Code Review
date: 2026-01-14
lede: For years I reviewed code the way I was reviewed. It took a bad quarter to notice that neither of us had ever asked what the review was for.
---

I used to be proud of my reviews. Thorough, line by line, every nit flagged. A pull request with forty comments felt like a job well done. Then a colleague told me, kindly, that people had started batching their changes into bigger PRs to get reviewed by me less often.

That is the opposite of what a review is supposed to do.

### The job of a review

A review exists to answer one question: is this change safe to merge, and will the next person be able to work with it? Everything else is optional. Style, naming, whether you would have done it differently, all of that is secondary and most of it should be handled by tooling or not at all.

Once I wrote that down, most of my forty comments disappeared. They were not about safety or the next person. They were about me.

### What I do now

- Read the description first and decide what I am worried about before reading the code
- Leave at most one comment per concern, not one per occurrence
- Mark anything that is a preference as a preference, and never block on it
- If I cannot find a real concern in ten minutes, I approve and say why

The last one was the hardest. An approval with "looks good, I checked the error handling and the migration" is a better review than twenty nits, because it tells the author what was actually verified.

### The thing nobody says out loud

> A review is a conversation between two people who both want the change to ship.

If either side forgets that, the review turns into a performance. The author performs diligence by addressing every comment. The reviewer performs rigor by producing them. Nobody is thinking about the user who will hit the bug neither of them looked for.

### Where this breaks down

This does not work on teams where review is the only gate. If there are no tests, no linting and no staging environment, the reviewer has to be a human linter, and the forty comments come back. The fix there is not a better reviewer. It is a pipeline that makes the reviewer's job smaller.

I still catch myself writing the nit. Most days I delete it before pressing send.
