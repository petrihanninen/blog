---
title: Why Every Team Reinvents the Ticket
lede: Draft. Half an argument about why issue trackers all converge on the same shape and why we keep rebuilding them anyway.
---

Every team I have worked with has, at some point, built their own issue tracker. A spreadsheet, a Notion board, a Slack channel with a naming convention. The commercial tool is always there in the background, paid for and half-used.

I do not think this is a tooling problem.

### The ticket is a boundary object

Rough notes, to be expanded:

- A ticket means different things to the person writing it, the person doing it, and the person reporting on it
- Every tool optimises for one of those three
- The team reinvents the ticket to serve the one the tool ignored
- This is fine and should probably be embraced rather than fought

### Open questions

Is there a tool that serves all three? Does it matter? Is the reinvention itself the useful part, the way writing a design doc is more useful than the doc?

More to come, maybe.
